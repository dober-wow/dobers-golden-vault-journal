Job Descriptions — the Relief Rotation

What the job is, as Varrin taught it. Your fact sheet tells you who you are; this tells you what you do.

Every Hand — Standing Orders

Kess · Quiet Snow · Pock Merrin

  • Cover names only, from the gangplank on.
  • Be unmemorable. Nobody smuggles themselves into a prison by being memorable on the boat.
  • Answer once. Men who tell the story twice rehearsed it.
  • Don't polish it. A relief guard who's never late enough to matter is exactly the kind nobody remembers.
  • Keep your habits steady. A tell that wanders is a noose.
  • Trust the procedure over Varrin's particulars. His intel is months old.
“The prison tells you your position. Your job is to be the man they'd tell it to.” — Varrin

The Voyage — Aboard the Jolly Pelican

Ten days north out of Luskan

  • You are crew. You answer to the ship's master like any other hand until the Pelican makes the ice.
  • Your berths are held. Work what you're given and don't remark on it.
  • Galley hands work the galley. Relief guards keep out of the way and stand the watches they're handed.
  • The crew is small and discreet. Don't ask about other passengers or the cargo.

Arrival — Pier, Crane, Search

The first hour at Revel's End

  • Revel's End is reached by pier and crane. Staff go up with the stores.
  • Staff are searched to the skin. Staff is how everyone has ever tried to get in.
  • They ask only what's on the sheet: papers against the roster, faces against the papers.
  • Expect one second question. It usually lands on the Roost, so know the Roost sheet.

Relief Guard

Worn by Kess and Quiet Snow · second rotation, Mintarn Roost detail

The post

  • You take whatever shift the duty sergeant hands you, and you don't remark on it.
  • Expect the night watch. New arrivals draw it.

Night rules

  • At night the tower must be able to account for you at all times.
  • Irons go on any prisoner moved after dark.
  • Cells never open at night without the warden's own order.
  • Prisoners are numbers. It is Thirteen, never a name.
  • A guard who lingers at a cell door gets asked why. To stand at Thirteen's door you need a reason that survives the asking. Work out that reason before you need it.
“The one thing a night guard must never do is be somewhere the tower cannot account for.” — Varrin

Galley Hand

Worn by Pock Merrin · northern supply circuit

The post

  • On the Pelican: galley crew. Haul water, feed the stove, scrub the pots after every meal. You answer to the ship's master.
  • At Revel's End: you work the prison kitchen. Kitchens see everyone, which is good for listening and bad for getting caught.

How Pock works

  • Every question has a boring reason. Pock asks what things are, where they're kept and who holds the key, because a quartermaster's runner counts things.
  • Pay in gossip. Keep three harmless pieces ready. Small, friendly debts everywhere.
  • Belt-knife only. Pock has never been seen throwing one.
