The Drill — Aboard the Cormorant

Varrin runs the mess table every night of the crossing. The ink is tested before it dries — and the corrections come in character, without warning. Never Varrin teaching. Only the world, rehearsing its disbelief.

The Table Rules — All Hands

As Varrin laid them down, mess table, first night out

On the boat

"Until she makes the ice, you answer to her master like any other crew. Nobody smuggles themselves into a prison by being memorable on the boat."
  • Cover names from here forward. Study them on the crossing — long enough to learn a name well enough to answer to it in your sleep.
  • Where an answer snags, the correction is cold and in character — a raised eyebrow, a single hard tap on the wood, wrong said the way a door shuts.

Inside the walls — what Varrin's intel buys (months old, bought at a distance)

  • Shifts: relief guards take whatever the duty sergeant hands them — and new arrivals get the night watch, because nobody senior wants it.
  • Night is the tower's hours: fewer bodies on the floor, but the sightlines never close. The one thing a night guard must never do is be somewhere the tower cannot account for.
  • After dark: irons go on any prisoner moved. Cells are never opened at night short of the warden's own order. A guard who lingers at a cell door gets asked why.
THE THIRTEEN PROBLEM — "If you mean to talk to Thirteen on the night watch, you will need a reason to be standing there that survives the asking." That reason is the whole job. Nothing else in this book matters if you don't have it.

Kess — Of the Mintarn Roost

Kenku · relief guard · three years on the rotation, according to this ink

The paper

Genuine, and it slides across the boards without ceremony. Three years on the Mintarn Roost rotation. The ink says so; your answers had better.

What the quizzing sounds like

"Kess. Service history — quick, now. Who trained you? ... And what's the Roost smell like, come winter? You'd know it in your sleep, if you'd stood a watch there."

The drill is not the roster line — it is the things only a body that stood there would hold: the smell of the Roost in winter, the name of the sergeant who ran you, the boredom of a third year on the same walls. Build the memories until they answer themselves.

Your liability

You are the only kenku this coast has seen, and the Varkenbluff posters sketch one. Crew, dockhands, clerks — they will look, every scene, even when nothing comes of it. The legend exists to give that noticing an answer: the name Kess must be standing there before the question arrives. Borrowed voices stay stowed unless the legend would use them.

NEVER — be memorable on the boat. A kenku is memorable enough.

Marta Greel — The Clerk's Error

Human · relief guard · answers only to "Greel," and always has

The paper

Genuine. "Marta Greel. Human, relief guard — a clerk's error is why she goes by 'Greel' on the manifest and always has. Nobody at Revel's End will think twice."

What the quizzing sounds like

"Marta Greel. A clerk's error put you in a guard's coat — so how does a clerk's error survive the muster roll? Last posting. Watch rotation — dawn or dusk?"

The error is the legend's soft spot and Varrin drills it first: the story must hold not just the name but how it survived — who signed off, which posting, which rotation you stood. Know the answer to "dawn or dusk" the way you know your own hands.

Your liability

Someone will read "Marta" off the roster and look up. The answer is a tired shrug from a man who stopped fighting the ministry's paperwork years ago — once, and never twice. Men who tell the story twice are men who rehearsed it.

NEVER — flinch at the name. It has been yours for years. Be bored by it.

Neris Fane — The Name Still Under the Knife

Moon-elf · relief guard · paperless until Luskan — the forgery is still being cut

The paper

The packet's one pure forgery, cut by Bethra's people when the roster changed at the Bridge Gate. No living owner behind the name — so no record can contradict it, and none can back it. Until Luskan, you sail carrying nothing but the name itself. Learn it deepest of the three: the paper will have to catch up to you.

What the drill sounds like — he saves the hardest scrutiny for last

"Now you. ... Hood down when you speak — a guard doesn't hide her face at table. Gloves off. Eyes up, not down: you're not ashamed, you're bored. [flat] Your name."

This is the discipline that matters: hiding is the tell. A relief guard among crew has nothing to conceal — face open, hands bare, gaze level and uninterested. Save the hood and gloves for the wind and the watch, where every soul on deck wears them too; at any table, under any question, you are simply bored.

Your liability

The amber-gold eyes, and what crackles about you when the blood is up — anyone who holds your gaze too long reads it. So the gaze gives them nothing worth holding: level, brief, bored. And the other thing does not happen where it can be seen. Not once. There is no story that survives a witnessed surge.

NEVER — hide at the table. Concealment reads as shame and shame reads as guilt. Boredom is the only safe face.
