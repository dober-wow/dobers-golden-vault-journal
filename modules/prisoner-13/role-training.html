The Drill Book — Six Days Aboard the Cormorant

Varrin runs the mess table every night of the sail. Names first, then duties, then everything that can kill the name.

Night One — All Hands

Everyone, every night, before the role work begins

The name is the armor

  • Cover names only past the gangplank. Slip once at sea and the name dies there — Varrin corrects in character, without warning.
  • Inside the walls, prisoners are numbers. It is Thirteen, never a name. A real name spoken aloud makes the whole prison sit up.
  • Relief guards report to Yula Dargeria, head guard — a no-nonsense stickler. Give her nothing to notice: crisp answers, short words, papers ready.

The prison breathes

Revel's End has a temper. Quiet days, the halls are loose. Give it a reason — a wrong name, a face where it shouldn't be, spellwork in the open, a chat with a prisoner nobody cleared — and the walls tighten: more eyes, sharper questions, shorter patience. It cools only with time kept clean. If the horn sounds and the lights burn red, it is too late to be clever — stand still, obey, be boring.

The rhythm to live by

  • Morning: privy-bucket run — manacled prisoners carry waste out under escort. Escort duty is honest guard work; volunteer for it sparingly.
  • Midday: exercise rotations in the courtyard. Quiet talk in the yard escapes the tower's hearing.
  • Evening: shift change — eight-hour shifts, two of three off duty at any hour. The bustle covers movement.
  • Always: the mess hall is legal mingling for every cover. The panopticon tower watches the floor at all hours — assume it sees you.
  • Patrols: two guards off the guard room walk the hexagon corridor, yard, and armory — four minutes a round. Step aside, nod, keep walking.
NEVER — enter the armory. The thing that guards it knows every face on the rolls and attacks the ones it doesn't. No relief guard's face is on its rolls. There is nothing in that room worth the horn.

Kess — Vault Detail, Second Rotation

Gate & receiving — four years on the Mintarn harbour fort gate-watch. Knows protocol cold; lets it show.

Your ground

  • The dock and the lift. Ships land at the cliff-base pier; everything and everyone rides the crane elevator up the hundred-sixty feet — one minute a car, crane worked by lever from the guard room. You know this machinery the way a gate-watch knows a portcullis: who rides, who signs, who waits.
  • Receiving. Papers are presented intact and unmarked to the officer of the watch. You have stood the other side of that exchange for four years — be the man who has seen a thousand chits and is impressed by none.
  • Escort work. Bucket run, courtyard rotation, movement of bodies between rooms. Manacles, spacing, eyes forward. This is your legal reason to be near prisoners.

Say it like the legend

"Orders travel. Rolls follow. If your name's not before me, you wait where I can see you."

Your liability

You are the only kenku this coast has ever seen, and the Varkenbluff posters sketch one. Every dockhand and clerk will look twice. The legend is the answer to the look: a gate-watch veteran is used to being stared at and stares back with a duty roster. Borrowed voices stay stowed unless the legend itself would use them.

NEVER — be clever at the gate. A gate man who improvises is remembered. Protocol, protocol, protocol.

Marta Greel — Stores Escort, Second Rotation

Stores & tally, off the Luskan quays. Answers only to "Greel."

Your ground

  • The storeroom. Six months of stockpile in crates. Your trade is the count: what came off the lift, what the kitchen drew, what the manifest says against what the shelf holds. Count everything twice; sign short.
  • The kitchen. Six cooks under Chef Tiny Toulaine — hulking, jovial, carries a saucepan where the crossbow should be. He feeds the people who help him. Stores escort makes you his favorite kind of guard: the one who brings the flour on time.
  • The mess hall. Your tally rounds cross every cover's path three times a day without a single question raised. You are the crew's legal courier.

Say it like the legend

"Roster says Marta. Quill-drunk clerk wrote it, ministry won't unwrite it. It's Greel."

Your liability

The name itself. Someone will read "Marta" off the roster line and look up. The drill is a tired shrug, not a flinch — a man who stopped fighting the ministry's paperwork years ago. Steady hands, counted words; the paper fits you because you fit the paper.

NEVER — over-explain the name. One shrug. Men who tell the story twice are men who rehearsed it.

Neris Fane — Wall Watch, Second Rotation

Harbour Watch out of Neverwinter, rolls in transfer. Varrin drills her hardest of the three.

Your ground

  • The battlements. Tower ladders up, hatches locked behind you, three to a roof in cold-weather gear, rotating against the wind off the Sea of Moving Ice. Up top, everyone is hooded, gloved, and squinting — the cold makes your discipline invisible. The wall is the one post where you disappear.
  • Nights. Lights dim after dark; the wall rotates more often against the cold; the floor below runs thin. You learn the prison's night face better than anyone inside it.
  • The alarm. Horn and red light means full deployment and the watch sees everything — when it sounds, be exactly where the roster says Neris Fane stands, doing exactly what she's paid to do.

Say it like the legend

"Rolls ride a slower courier than I do. Transfers lag a season — ask Neverwinter. The chit's stamped."

Your liability

Your eyes. Amber-gold, and the air about you crackles when the blood is up — anyone who holds your gaze too long reads it as fey-touched. So nobody holds it: hood up, gloves on, eyes down, answers short. And the other thing does not happen where it can be seen. Not once. There is no story that survives a witnessed surge.

NEVER — let them check the rolls while you stand there. The story walks: name the courier, name the season, move along. If a clerk digs, Neris Fane is due on the wall and walks away politely.
